# Playtest, 2026-10-10

A new player's first three days on seed 7, and the opening again on
seed 3, played from a cloud container through the real client
(Playwright's Chromium, WebGPU in software) and the hand (`bun run act`),
at commit 07a722f. Pictures are in the session's scratchpad,
`playtest/`; names below are theirs.

## Diary

**Day 1, 00:16.** The game opens on a dark navy screen: it is a quarter
past midnight, and a day is twenty real minutes, so the island stays
dark for six. With the time forced to noon the first view is a field of
grass, nothing else; the minimap shows the coast thirty tiles north,
out of view (`01-opens-at-night`, `02-first-view-no-coast`).

The guide's first beat: "Build a harbour on the coast". *Take the
harbour* picks up the tool and leaves the camera where it is. I pan to
the coast: no dots anywhere, with the harbour in hand
(`03-harbour-tool-no-dots-anywhere`). Every building must be painted
beside a street, and there is none yet; the street is beat three. With
the hand I drafted a harbour on a coast tile; the bill said "a harbour,
costs nothing", Build turned it amber, "1 step no longer fits"
(`04-harbour-refused-no-longer-fits`), no reason given. Only after
drawing a street along the coast did the harbour's dots appear, and it
has to be painted three tiles by three, its back on the sea; that I
learned from `blueprint.rs`, not from the game. An inland harbour
painted beside a street is accepted, stands, and its card says "No
berth: the harbour's back is not on the sea" (`09-inland-harbour-accepted-dead`):
the hand's dots allow it, nothing stops it, and it is dead.

**Day 1, 22:41.** Harbour built. The first ferry was in at 23:11, half
an hour later, by night. Depot and street were quick: the depot stands
at once, free, and it was already joined by the street I had drawn for
the harbour, so beat three ticked itself. *Send the lorry* fetched a box
in about ten real seconds; it is satisfying to watch, the ferry at the
ramp, the tug, the boxes in the park in their colours
(`07-first-fetch-ferry-docked`).

**Day 2.** Twelve houses and their street: the bill said "48 timber ·
covered, 10 stone · covered" (`05-bill-says-stone-covered`). Build took
eleven coins (`06-after-build-12-coins-gone`): the stone was in the
trailer park, the road is laid at Build from the depots' shelves, and
the rest comes express. Houses went up fast, a row joins into one long
red roof, and the next ferries brought 24 settlers, whose cars park in
front. I turned on standing orders: it fetched the rest of the pack, one
box an hour or so (08:49 to 20:53 for five boxes, three tiles from the
depot), and it also bought two boxes of timber and two of crates I
never asked for, from the default rules.

The guide stayed at "4 of 5, Build houses" over a street of lived-in
houses: each look was a fresh browser, and the guide ticks the last
beat only if it watched the ferry come in with settlers
(`10-day3-guide-stuck-4of5`).

**Day 3.** Sawmill at the forest's edge, thirty tiles off, on a road of
32 tiles: the bill said "31 stone · 11 short, Order 1 box −11". The road
was laid at Build and eleven coins went express anyway; the box would
have landed hours later. My drag clipped the depot's corner, so the road
was laid unjoined, red, and paid for; joining it took four tries against
the rule that two arms may not meet at 45°. Two shops and a farm: the
shops filled with 80 crates and nobody ate there, everyone eats at home.
The farm's fields are huge and wrap round the streets.

**Day 4.** Coins 500 → 372, no income: everything spent went to express
stone and to default top-up rules. GDP comes from the farm and the
sawmill; the level ring went from 0 to 12 in a day and unlocks nothing.
One box of 100 crates, bought by a rule, has stood in the park since
day 3: the depot has 120 of 200, the lorry fetches only a box that fits
whole, and nothing draws the crates down. An apartment without stone:
its card is the best thing in the game, "No stone in town, and none
coming; 1 box on the next ferry, −11 coins; Order stone; the depot
keeps no stone of its own, Keep 20 in stock"
(`11-stalled-site-card-good`).

**Seed 3.** Same first view, coast out of sight. With the fixes below:
the town opens at nine, *Take the street* flies to the coast
(`12-fixed-guide-flies-to-coast`); harbour, street and depot drafted
together, built in one press; ferry in twenty minutes later, tug
working the boxes off in daylight. Tapping the lorry, as the guide
says, opened a card with its fuel and nothing to press, and folded the
guide's own Send button away (`08-lorry-card-nothing-to-press`); fixed
(`14-fixed-lorry-card-sends`).

## The three changes that would most improve it

1. **Let the opening be done without reading the code.** Even with the
   new wording, a first harbour is the hardest thing in the game: a 3×3
   plot, painted, back to the sea, beside a street, on a coast the
   camera does not show. A harbour tool that previews its whole plot
   under the pointer, turned to the sea, green where it would berth and
   red where it would not, and lays its own stub of street, would make
   beat one a single click. The hand already knows the terrain; it needs
   the berth rule (`berth_at`) mirrored as it mirrors the rest.

2. **Make the purse move both ways, and give the level a meaning.** By
   day four the coins only fall, the level climbs without consequence,
   and shops sit full because everyone eats at home. One pull would do:
   the world buying what the town makes as a standing line the player
   sets (the depot's "sell over" exists but nothing points at it), so a
   farm or sawmill earns coins you can see land; and the next building
   kind, or a second lorry, priced in level rather than given. The
   stalled-site card shows how good the game is at saying "here is what
   is short and here is the button".

3. **Never spend the player's coins without the player's sentence.**
   Express road stone and the default top-up rules are both reasonable,
   but both were invisible until the purse moved: the bill now says the
   first; the second should be said on the toggle and in the news ("the
   depot ordered 100 crates: under its keep line of 80, −44"), and the
   default rules could start off, drawn but unset, until the player
   turns standing orders on. A town that buys things on its own is
   automation the player has not yet earned.
