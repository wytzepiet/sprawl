# Resident behaviour: a utility model over time-denominated needs

Status: built, 2026-09-04, and `game.md` is the current shape. The needs
are home, work, rest, eat and the car's fuel; leisure went with milestone
0's cut. Nothing in town has a price, so a visit scores its time alone.
Since 2026-09-09 the bucket is a stock (§3.1). The open questions, the
validation and the build plan this was built from last stood at
`53a9ca6`. Supersedes the agenda/entry sections of `archive/agents.md`.

---

## 1. Scope

This document specifies how an agent in Sprawl decides where to go next. It
covers the decision procedure, the data it reads, the properties claimed for
it, and the assumptions that have not been tested. It does not specify
routing, vehicle physics, or the building-proposal system, though it names
the interfaces to them.

The design target is a discrete-event simulation in which agents wake, read
world state, decide, and schedule their next wake. No agent holds a plan.
Any wake at any time must converge on the same behaviour, so that duplicate
wakes are harmless and no stored intention can go stale.

## 2. Problem statement

An earlier design gave each resident a stored agenda of entries, each with a
window, a duration, a priority and a miss-handling rule. That design required
booking (choosing when a flexible entry occurs), feasibility checking,
conflict resolution by priority, one step of lookahead to protect
commitments, and an escalation ladder for missed entries.

The model below replaces those mechanisms with a scalar comparison. The
claim under review is that the replacement is smaller and that the deleted
behaviours re-emerge as consequences rather than as rules.

## 3. Model

### 3.1 Buckets

A **need** is a category (Home, Work, Rest, Eat, Fuel). Each need has three
static parameters:

- `fill` — units accrued per unit time
- `cap` — maximum accumulation

A **bucket** is an instance of a need held by an entity, carrying a `level`
in the same units.

Units are **hours of obligation**. A bucket's level is the amount of time the
holder owes that activity.

`fill` is a rate applied only while the bucket is not discharging (invariant
B1), so the daily total is emergent rather than declared. For a target of `D`
hours per day at discharge rate `r`, the equilibrium is reached at

```
fill = r * D / (24 - D)
```

Sleep at 8 hours per day at unit rate gives `fill = 0.5`. The equilibrium is
stable: a longer discharge leaves less time to accrue. `fill` is therefore
derivable from an external referent (time-use data) rather than tuned to it.

This derivation holds per need in isolation — it assumes the bucket gets its
`D` hours. Nothing in the procedure guarantees the authored set fits in a
day, and if `sum(D) > 24` every resident is permanently in deficit for a
reason no player could diagnose.

**Invariant N1.** `sum over accruing needs of D <= 24`, asserted at
startup. Constant needs fill time rather than demanding it.

**Constant buckets.** A need whose timing is imposed by the world rather than
generated by the passage of time — Work, School — is a bucket with `fill = 0`
and `level = k * cap` for a chosen `k` in (0, 1]. It never fills and, being
large relative to any one visit, never empties: the tap's curve alone decides
when it is served. Under section 4.1 its weight is the constant `k`, which
reads as a threshold — the agent goes to work unless something else is more
than `k` overdue — and the same `k` decides how overdue something must be to
pull the agent out mid-shift. This is a parameterisation, not a second kind
of bucket; no code path distinguishes it.

The dichotomy is the rule for adding a need: **accruing** if its urgency is
generated by time (`fill` carries the timing), **constant** if its timing is
imposed by the world (`curve` carries it). A constant bucket neither accrues
nor drains: section 4.3 leaves it alone.

`archive/economy.md` §4 adds a third kind and flips the sign. A **hazard** bucket
owes nothing until a random draw with a rate fills it (illness, fire),
and it is the one kind with a consequence at the end of its range: a
resident dies, a building is rubble. And the bucket becomes a **stock** that runs down
rather than an obligation that fills, so that a shelf, a tank and an
appetite are one struct; every formula here holds with `level` read as
`cap − stock`, and `fill` as the rate of use.

**Home** is a constant need served always at the residence, at `k = 0.3`.
It is the baseline every other activity has to beat, and what pulls a
resident back when the shift ends and nothing else is open yet. Its `k` is
bounded on both sides: above what waiting overnight at work scores
(`k_work * shift / 24`, about 0.19 for a nine-hour shift), below what a
morning's commute scores (`k_work * shift / (tau + shift)`).

Buckets are held by whichever entity has the need. This is normally a
resident, but may be a household or a building (section 8.1).

### 3.2 Taps

A building publishes zero or more **taps**. A tap serves one need and
carries:

- `curve` — availability `a(t)` in [0,1], defined over a day, periodic
- `rate` — `r > 0`, units discharged per unit time at full availability;
  may be positive infinity
- `overhead` — `h >= 0`, time cost incurred on arrival before service begins

Effective discharge rate at time `t` is `r * a(t)`.

`curve` and `rate` are separated so that `curve` remains finite and
integrable while `rate` may be infinite. An infinite rate denotes a service
whose duration is fixed regardless of how much is owed.

**Invariant T1.** A tap with `rate = infinity` must have `overhead > 0`.
Section 5.1 depends on this.

### 3.3 Curves

A curve is a list of keyframes `(time_of_day, value)`, linearly interpolated,
wrapping at the day boundary. Each keyframe additionally stores the integral
from the first keyframe to itself, computed once when the curve is built.

Three operations are required:

- `A(x, y)` — the integral of `a` over `[x, y]`. Two lookups, a subtraction,
  plus whole days between.
- `advance(x, q)` — the least `t >= x` with `A(x, t) = q`, or none. Requires
  solving a quadratic on the segment where the integral runs out.
- `next_zero(x)`, `next_nonzero(x)` — segment scans.

Curves are built at startup from fixed definitions. Lazy construction is
prohibited (section 9).

A curve that is nowhere zero (for example, a residence's Rest tap) makes
`next_zero` unbounded. A horizon cap of one day is applied. This is sound
only while no bucket can take longer than a day to discharge.

**Invariant C1.** For every accruing need and every tap serving it,
`cap / r <= 1 day`, asserted at startup. A constant need at a tap that
never closes is fine: `overtake` (section 4.2) ends the visit, not the
curve. A hazard need (`archive/economy.md` §4) may take longer than a day to
refill — a hospital stay — and is the reason the horizon cap will have
to become per need.

`next_zero` means "closed from here for a while", not "touches zero here":
the foot of an opening ramp is where `next_nonzero` lands, and it must not
also be where `next_zero` lands, or the interval between them is empty.

## 4. Decision procedure

### 4.1 Evaluating one option

An option is a pair (bucket `b`, building `B`). Let `L` be the bucket level,
`tau` the estimated travel time from the agent's current position to `B`,
and `(a, r, h)` the tap's parameters. The bucket's weight is its fullness:

```
w = L / cap
```

This is what makes level order needs against each other (section 5.5). It
is bounded by 1, so every bound in section 5 survives it, and it carries no
parameter of its own. A large override weight (section 7) is the only
exception.

```
opening   = next_nonzero(now + tau + h)          none: yields nothing
departure = max(now, opening - h - tau)
entry     = departure + tau + h
dry       = next_zero(entry)                     capped at entry + one day
available = A(entry, dry)                        availability-hours ahead
```

If `available <= 0` the option yields nothing.

```
drained   = min(L, r * available)                r = infinity yields L
```

If `drained` is below the clock's resolution the option yields nothing.

```
leave     = advance(entry, drained / r)          r = infinity yields entry
score     = w * drained / (leave - now)
```

The option yields `Go(score, departure, leave)`.

The denominator is everything from now until the visit is over: the wait
for the tap to open, the journey, the overhead, the service. Waiting is
priced, not special-cased. Nobody sets out early to wait somewhere else —
section 4.2 keeps an option whose `departure` is later from being acted on
— but someone already at the place is scored on waiting there honestly, and
does not drive home for five minutes because the estimate ran early.

Note that `drained` is evaluated over the interval the agent would actually
be present, not instantaneously at arrival. Arriving shortly before a tap
closes is scored on the little that remains.

### 4.2 Selection

```
ceiling(b) = w * min(R(need), L / H(need))
```

where `R` is the greatest rate and `H` the least overhead among taps serving
that need. Invariant T1 makes this finite. `ceiling` bounds `score` over all
options for that bucket (section 5.1) and requires no travel estimate.

An option is **actionable** if it can be started now: its `departure` is
now, or the agent is already at its building, where waiting for it is the
action. An option that is neither is a wait to be spent elsewhere; it
contributes its `departure` as an alarm and nothing else.

`candidates(b)` is every building standing that has a tap for the need —
the buildings of the surveyed chunks, and the **edge**: a building at each
road exit, serving every need in the game, never closed and never full. So
a bucket with nothing in town is never a bucket with nothing at all; it is
a bucket whose only option is a long drive, and it scores like one. `Go`
with a poor score, rather than `Nothing`, is the normal answer to a town
that has not built the thing yet.

```
best = none                                     score(none) = 0

for b in buckets ordered by ceiling descending:
    if ceiling(b) <= score(best): break
    for B in candidates(b):
        case evaluate(b, B):
            Go(s, ..) if actionable : if s > score(best): best = (B, s)
            otherwise               : skip

if best.departure > now, or best is where the agent already is:
    remain
otherwise:
    travel there

schedule wake at min(unseat(best), overtake(best))
```

`unseat(best)` is `departure` if that is still ahead, and otherwise `leave`:
the earlier of the chosen bucket emptying and the chosen tap closing. Both
are closed-form on piecewise-linear data.

`overtake(best)` is the earliest instant at which any bucket could possibly
outrank the choice. It is the pruning ceiling inverted over time. A bucket's
level grows linearly at `fill` while it is not being served, so

```
ceiling_b(t) = (L + fill * t) / cap * min(R, (L + fill * t) / H)
```

is monotone in `t`. But the ceiling is loose — it ignores travel and
waiting — and the gap between it and the real score *closes* as the bucket
fills, so any estimate from the ceiling alone is late, and late can be
never. The real score is solved instead: with `T` the fixed part of the
denominator (wait, travel, overhead) and the whole level served,

```
(L / cap) * L / (T + L / r) = score(best)
```

is a quadratic in `L`, closed-form, and exact except that a shrinking wait
makes it late by at most the wait, which the departure term caps. A bucket
with no option at all falls back to the ceiling. One already past the
crossing and still not winning is not held back by level, and gets no
level term.

A bucket that cannot cross by level — `fill = 0`, or already at `cap` — can
only become actionable through a tap opening, and that is its `departure`
term. `overtake` is the minimum over all buckets, including those pruned by
the break above.

This is the lookahead section 2 deleted, recovered as a bound. No plan is
stored, a duplicate wake is harmless, and the agent is woken exactly when
the answer could change and not before. It also guarantees a finite wake:
`best = none` means every bucket's term is a curve transition, and a world
with no taps at all is the unmet-demand case of section 6, which is
reported at this point rather than detected separately.

Every option therefore returns either a destination or nothing. The
procedure answers "what should be done now" and never constructs a schedule.

The ceiling-ordered break is a performance measure (section 10) and is not
built until there are enough buckets for it to matter.

### 4.3 Bucket dynamics

**Invariant B1.** A bucket accrues only while it is not discharging.

This is required because `stay` is computed as though `level` were static
during the visit. Where fill and discharge overlap, the estimate is wrong by
the ratio between them; for Work (8 hours per day against a discharge rate of
1) the error would be roughly 50%.

Discharge is determined by **the activity the agent selected**, not by which
taps exist where it is standing. A residence may publish an always-open Rest
tap, but an agent at home pursuing Eat is not discharging Rest and must
accrue it. The selected need is therefore agent state, in the same category
as the agent's location: a record of what is happening, not an intention.

Settlement is lazy and occurs only at wakes. The served bucket accrues in
proportion to the tap's *unavailability* over the interval, so a shift that
ends partway through, or a tap that tails off, needs no boundary handling:

```
settle(agent, now):
    for b in agent.buckets:
        if b.fill == 0: continue                 # constant: constant
        if b.need == agent.selected and agent is at the selected building:
            served = A(last_update, now)            # availability-hours
        else:
            served = 0
        idle = (now - last_update) - served         # unavailability-hours

        b.level = max(0, b.level - rate * served)
        b.level = min(b.cap, b.level + fill * idle)

    agent.last_update = now
```

At full availability this is B1 exactly; at zero it is full accrual; between,
it interpolates. Section 4.1's `stay` ignores the accrual term and is
therefore optimistic under partial availability by at most
`fill * (dry - entry)`. The error is bounded and self-correcting: the agent
wakes at `unseat`, finds the bucket non-empty, and stays.

## 5. Claimed properties

These are asserted from the algebra, not from observation.

### 5.1 Boundedness

`drained <= r * (leave - entry)` and `stay >= (leave - entry)`, so

```
score <= w * r
```

and separately `drained <= L` with `stay >= h`, so `score <= w * L / h`.
The score cannot diverge as travel or stay approach zero. This bound is also
the pruning ceiling in 4.2.

### 5.2 Plateau during service

While an agent is present and availability is constant, `tau = 0` and
`drained / stay` equals `r` exactly, independent of `L`. The score is
therefore flat for the duration of a visit and falls discontinuously when the
bucket empties or the curve reaches zero.

Under `w = L / cap` the score is not flat: it falls as the bucket drains.
What prevents an in-progress activity being abandoned for a marginally
better alternative is not flatness but *sampling* — the agent is only woken
at `unseat` or `overtake`, and `overtake` is a bound on ceilings, not on
scores. Two co-located taps whose scores cross mid-visit do not cause a
wake, so there is no chatter and no hysteresis term.

### 5.3 Departure timing

Because an option is not actionable until `opening - h - tau`, an agent does not
set out earlier than necessary to be served on arrival. No waiting penalty or
patience constant is required.

### 5.4 Truncation and infeasibility

A partial window yields a proportionally reduced `drained`, and a window that
has closed yields none. Truncated attendance and refusal to set out are the
same expression evaluated at different times.

### 5.5 Level orders needs; rate ranks them

`score <= (L / cap) * r`. A fuller bucket outranks an emptier one at equal
rate; a constant bucket at `k` is outranked exactly when something else is
more than `k` full. Escalation is bounded — no score exceeds `r` — so the
override threshold in section 7 remains computable, but within the bound
the ordering is decided by level rather than fixed by parameters.

The bound is also the design surface for what may interrupt what: a need
whose greatest rate is below a constant need's `k` can never pull an agent
away from it, however full. The rates therefore form a ladder — Rest 1,
Eat 1, Work 0.5, Home 0.3 — and `cap`
cannot substitute for it: at full, a bucket scores its tap's rate whatever
the cap is.

Without the fullness term, `sup over L of score = r` and the ordering
between two available needs would be fixed for all time by `r` alone;
hunger would make an agent drive farther but never eat first. That was the
original form and it was rejected on this ground.

### 5.6 Pressure enters through travel amortisation

With `tau > 0`, `score = (L / cap) * L / (tau + h + L/r)` is increasing in
`L` on both counts. A fuller bucket justifies a longer journey.

Consequence: agents batch errands. A constant bucket is the deliberate
exception: its level never changes, so its commute tolerance is authored via
`k` rather than emergent. Job commitment is not a function of how behind
one is.

## 6. Interfaces

- **Travel estimation.** The procedure requires `tau` for each option
  considered. It does not specify how. See section 10.
- **Routing.** On deciding to travel, a route is computed by existing
  pathfinding. The estimate used for the decision and the route used for the
  journey are distinct.
- **Output.** The total discharged by a building's taps over a period is the
  service it delivered — for a workplace, labour received.

## 7. Parameterisation

Four places carry behaviour, each with an external referent:

| parameter | meaning | source |
|---|---|---|
| `fill` | daily time demand of the activity | time-use data |
| `curve` | when, and how effectively, a place serves | opening hours; physiology |
| `cap` | how much may accumulate | domain fact |
| `k` | threshold below which a constant need is skipped | judgement, in bucket units |
| `rate` | a need's importance at its most urgent | judgement: the ladder in section 5.5 |

Weight is fullness (section 4.1) and is not authored. Rate is, and it is
the one place needs are ranked against each other. An override (dispatch,
emergency) is a weight exceeding 1; section 5.1 makes the threshold
computable rather than empirical: any weight exceeding the greatest ordinary
`r` guarantees dominance.

The discipline under review is that a behavioural error should first be
attributed to `curve`, `fill` or `cap`, and only to `k` when a one-sentence
justification in terms of "how overdue must something be to skip this" can
be given.

## 8. Extensions outside the core

None are required by the core procedure. Each is listed with the condition
that would motivate building it.

### 8.1 Pools

A bucket may be visible to more than one agent. Membership is drawn from an
existing relationship (residents of a home, employees of a building, a
vehicle's owner), so no membership registry is introduced. A personal need is
a pool of one.

Claiming is a lease — an annotation on the bucket naming the claimant and an
expiry — rather than a transfer. A lapsed claim requires no cleanup path.

Motivating condition: a second agent could perform the same task.

### 8.2 Preconditions

Some options require a state to hold before they can be served: a passenger
aboard, a load aboard, a particular vehicle, fuel. These are resolved in the
journey layer by backward chaining into additional legs, and `tau` becomes
the sum over legs.

Constraints: depth-capped, acyclic, and never stored. Recomputation against
world state at every decision is what makes partial failure recoverable.

Cargo is a **role in a journey**, not a class of entity. A transported
resident remains a resident throughout.

Motivating condition: an option that cannot be served by travelling directly
to one building.

### 8.3 Buildings as agents

A building may hold a task pool, a fleet, and a dispatch rule: if a vehicle
is free and sufficient staff are present, claim the best open task and send a
vehicle. Vehicles run a three-state script (depart, serve, return).

This substitutes for modelling service-vehicle drivers as deciding agents.
Staffing remains real, since employees commute under the core procedure, and
therefore congestion still determines service capacity.

Motivating condition: emergency services or freight.

### 8.4 Ownership determines the decider

Where a need is shared, the bucket is held by the shared entity, which is
then the deciding agent. This avoids inter-agent negotiation for group
activities. Unverified.

## 9. Determinism requirements

The simulation must be reproducible across runs with a fixed seed.

- Equal scores must break on a stable key (bucket index, then building id).
  Floating-point comparison of independently computed scores may tie.
- Curve tables must be constructed at startup from fixed definitions.
- Any cached or lazily materialised structure must be keyed and refreshed on
  simulation time, never wall-clock time or iteration order.

A prior defect in this codebase — non-reproducible traffic caused by
iteration over a hash map — is the reference case.

## 10. Performance model

Per wake, the intended cost is:

1. Settle buckets: one pass, arithmetic only.
2. Compute ceilings: one multiply per bucket, no travel estimate.
3. Descend in ceiling order, stopping when the ceiling falls below the best
   score found. Most buckets are expected to terminate here.
4. For surviving buckets, evaluate candidates. Each evaluation is a bounded
   number of keyframe operations.

Two further reductions are specified but not required:

- **Radius bound.** A candidate cannot win if `w * L / tau` is already below
  the best score, giving a search radius that contracts as good options are
  found.
- **Deferred refinement.** Score candidates with an optimistic travel
  estimate; recompute with an accurate one only where the ranking is in
  doubt. Correctness is preserved if the optimistic estimate never
  understates the score; with a non-monotonic curve this does not hold
  exactly, and the resulting error is bounded by the curve's slope.

Travel estimation is unresolved. Candidates considered: straight-line
distance with a detour factor (in use today); per-agent memoised route
estimates; per-origin Dijkstra fields over the contracted junction graph,
materialised on use and invalidated by chunk edit stamps. The last was
analysed and deferred as unmotivated at current scale.
